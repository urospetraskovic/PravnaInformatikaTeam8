CASE 1: K 217/2024 - BERANAMA
================================================================================
Court: Osnovni Sud u Beranama
Case Number: K 217/2024
Date of Verdict: 07. Maj 2025
Judge: Došljak V. Ivan
Verdict Date (Published): 09.05.2025

PERPETRATOR DATA:
- Name: Ć.S.
- JMBG: [REDACTED]
- Position: Deputy School Director (pomoćnik direktora)
- Age/Background: Family man with one child
- Education: VSS (higher education)
- Prior Criminal Record: YES
  * Conviction 1: 05.12.2001 - Article 039 KZ RCG - Fine 5,000 dinars
  * Conviction 2: 14.05.2004 - Article 190 KZ RCG - 2 months conditional jail (1 year)
- Financial Status: Middle class (srednje imovinsko stanje)
- Literacy: Yes (pismen)

VICTIM DATA:
- Name: B.S.
- Position: Teacher (učitelj)
- Workplace: JU OŠ "V.K." (Public School)
- Status: Colleague of perpetrator

INCIDENT DETAILS:
- Date: October 16, 2024 (~15:50)
- Location: Entrance to JU OŠ "V.K." in Berana
- Incident Context: School setting, during break time

INCIDENT TRIGGER:
- Victim was smoking at main entrance (prohibited area)
- Perpetrator attempted to pass, asked victim to move
- Victim objected to manner of request
- Escalated to verbal conflict

HARASSMENT TYPE:
- Verbal harassment and threats
- Aggressive body language
- Multiple repeated threats
- Spitting incident

SPECIFIC THREATS REPORTED:
1. "jebem ti majku" (sexual/profane curse)
2. "ološu jedan" (you bastard)
3. "šizofreničaru" (schizophrenic)
4. "napušiš mi se kurca" (crude sexual reference)
5. "usraću ti se u život" (extreme vulgar threat)
6. "narkomanu jedan" (drug addict)
7. "platićeš mi za sve" (you'll pay me)
8. "polomiću te" (I'll break you)
9. "izlomiću te" (I'll destroy you)
10. "nećeš živ ostat" (you won't stay alive)

THREAT CHARACTERISTICS:
- Repeated multiple times
- Escalating intensity
- Accompanied by aggressive body language (repeated lunging)
- Witness reports of perpetrator approaching victim aggressively 5-6 times

EVIDENCE:
- Video surveillance: YES - captures multiple approach attempts by perpetrator
- Witness testimony: Multiple witnesses present
  * School guard R.D. - physically intervened to prevent physical contact
  * Teacher R.M. - present at incident
  * Other staff present
- Victim statement: Clear and detailed account
- Incident corroboration: Video shows repeated lunging motions toward victim
- Physical evidence: Spitting occurred (per victim statement)

POWER DYNAMICS:
- Perpetrator: Deputy school director (supervisory position)
- Victim: Teacher (subordinate position)
- Authority imbalance: YES - superior threatening subordinate
- Workplace hierarchy violation: YES

LABOR LAW VIOLATIONS:
- Article 47 of Labor Code Agreement (mobing): Psychological harassment/humiliation
- Disciplinary proceedings: School initiated disciplinary review
- School finding: Both perpetrator and victim initially found responsible under Article 47
  * Victim finding: LATER REVERSED - Found NOT responsible (Decision 29.11.2024)
  * Perpetrator finding: GUILTY - 30% salary deduction for 2 months (Decision 29.11.2024)

VERDICT OUTCOME:
- Finding: GUILTY (Okrivljeni je proglašen krivim)
- Charge: Article 168.st.1 KZ CG (Endangering Security/Ugrožavanje sigurnosti)
- Sentence: €600 fine (convertible to 24 days jail at €25/day)
- Court costs: €30
- Victim reparation: Referred to civil proceedings for damages
- Verdict Status: FINAL (pravosnažna)

LEGAL ANALYSIS:
- Court determined: All elements of Article 168 KZ CG satisfied
- Perpetrator mental state: Capable of understanding action, managing conduct
- Perpetrator awareness: Knew action was prohibited, acted intentionally
- Victim impact: Genuine fear and sense of insecurity caused
- Threat assessment: Court found threats serious and objectively similar to threatening attack
- Relevance of prior convictions: Considered as aggravating factor
- Mitigating factors: Family man status (father of one)

ARTICLE 168 KZ CG APPLICATION:
The court applied Article 168 st.1 which addresses threatening security with potential attack on life/body
- Requirement: Threats must cause fear and insecurity in victim
- Assessment: Court found objective danger present even without physical harm
- Perpetrator intent: Court found willing achievement of threatening consequence

PSYCHOLOGICAL IMPACT:
- Victim: Ongoing fear and sense of danger ("taj osjećaj ga i dalje drži")
- Victim self-reporting: Continues to feel threatened and unsafe
- Work environment: Damaged professional relationship; no longer communicate

SCHOOL DISCIPLINE CONTEXT:
- Disciplinary procedures: Conducted parallel to criminal proceedings
- Initial assessment: Both perpetrator and victim charged under mobing
- Resolution: Only perpetrator found guilty of mobing
- Victim exoneration: 29.11.2024 decision reversed victim's guilt finding
- Perpetrator discipline: Salary penalty imposed independently of criminal sentence

ADDITIONAL NOTES:
- Case represents classic workplace mobbing with supervisor-subordinate power dynamic
- Multiple witnesses corroborate victim's account
- Video evidence crucial in conviction
- Sentences both criminal and disciplinary occurred
- Appeal possibility: Standard 8-day appeal window to Higher Court (Viši sud u Bijelom Polju)

CASE 2: K 277/2012 - BIJELO POLJE
================================================================================
Court: Osnovni Sud u Bijelom Polju
Case Number: K 277/2012
Date of Verdict: 23. Oktobar 2012
Judge: Dragan Mrdak
Trial Type: ACQUITTAL CASE

PERPETRATOR DATA:
- Name: A.Z.
- JMBG: [REDACTED]
- Position: Restaurant owner (privatni ugostitelj)
- Place: Restoran "Krstac" - Mojkovac
- Marital Status: Married with two adult children
- Education: Middle school
- Prior Record: YES
  * Conviction: Opštinski sud Bijelo Polje 1987 - Article 136 KZ SRCG (illegal trade)
  * Sentence: 6 months conditional jail (1 year conditional)
- Financial Status: Middle class
- Ethnicity: Montenegrin

VICTIM DATA:
- Name: R.G.
- Position: Waitress/Server (konobarica)
- Workplace: Restaurant "Krstac" - Mojkovac
- Employment Status: Employee under A.Z.
- Employment Duration: 2 years at restaurant

INCIDENT DETAILS:
- Date: April 24, 2012
- Time: ~04:30 (early morning)
- Location: Restaurant "Krstac" - Mojkovac
- Incident Type: Post-incident confrontation with employee

INCIDENT TRIGGER:
- Previous evening: Drunken guest (I.I.) harassing Albanian/Kosovar customers
- Previous evening: Victim R.G. handled situation professionally - removed disruptive guest
- Critical night: Restaurant owner A.Z. heard about incident from guest
- Critical night: Owner involved in car accident while rushing to restaurant (hit banking)
- Critical night: Owner arrived at restaurant agitated and injured (head wound)

PERPETRATOR ACTIONS (Critical Morning):
1. Verbal abuse with profanity:
   - "nemaš na računu ... majku ti jebem"
   - "primate platu, nemate para da kupite gaće"
   - "sa takvim radom nećete imati ni gaća"
   - "bijede, nemate gaća"
   - "nemate na računu"

2. Physical restraint:
   - Locked victim in separate area (Separe) - demonstrating force
   - Threatened physical violence or mistreatment
   - Broke restaurant glassware/items

3. Physical assault:
   - Hit victim with closed fist on shoulder/arm area
   - Poured alcoholic drink (štok) in victim's face
   - Poured mineral water on victim
   - Threw multiple glasses from table (broke 3 glasses)
   - Broke flower pot with foot

4. Continued harassment:
   - Demanded victim bring new pot next day
   - Repeated verbal attacks for 5-10 minutes
   - Yelling and screaming throughout

HARASSMENT CONTEXT:
- Victim professional during previous incident (removed disruptive guest properly)
- Victim couldn't reach owner by phone immediately
- Victim had limited phone credit
- Victim's only "failure" was inability to immediately notify owner
- Incident occurred on victim's work shift

POWER DYNAMICS:
- Perpetrator: Restaurant owner (employer/boss)
- Victim: Employee/waitress (subordinate)
- Authority imbalance: YES - employer over employee
- Workplace hierarchy: Yes

LABOR LAW VIOLATIONS:
- Article 8a Zakon o radu (Law on Labor): Prohibition of mobbing/harassment
- Violation type: Workplace harassment with intent to cause fear, humiliation, damage professional integrity
- Victim impact: Psychological trauma, fear, humiliation
- Consequence: Victim voluntarily terminated employment (self-initiated resignation)

ADMINISTRATIVE/CRIMINAL FINDINGS:
- Misdemeanor court finding: GUILTY
- Misdemeanor charge: Article 10 st.2 Zakon o javnom redu i miru (Public Order Law)
- Misdemeanor finding date: May 31, 2012 (PP br.462/12)
- Misdemeanor sentence: €350 fine
- Misdemeanor description: Very rude conduct, insults, speech acts, physical assault

CRIMINAL VERDICT OUTCOME:
- Charge: Article 224 KZ CG (Violation of Labor Rights / Povreda prava iz rada)
- Criminal Finding: NOT GUILTY / ACQUITTED
- Verdict Date: October 23, 2012
- Acquittal Basis: Article 373 st.1 tač.2 ZKP (insufficient evidence)

ACQUITTAL REASONING:
The court determined that criminal charge (Article 224) differs from misdemeanor:
- Article 224 KZ CG requires PATTERN of harassment (ponavljanje)
- Single isolated incident insufficient for criminal conviction
- Misdemeanor court properly handled punishment
- Labor law violation requires repeated conduct over time
- First and only incident in 2-year employment relationship

PRIOR RELATIONSHIP:
- Employment duration: 2 years
- Prior conflicts: One minor disagreement 1 week before (not escalated)
- Victim statement: "no previous such problems"
- Witness confirmation: No prior harassment documented
- Work atmosphere: Previously cordial

VICTIM PSYCHOLOGICAL IMPACT:
- Immediate reaction: Strong stress, crying, unable to work
- Physical impact: Drink entered eye area and nose (minor injury)
- Mental health: "High stress", required colleague support
- Career consequence: Resigned from job same night/next morning
- Post-incident: Victim contemplated legal action

EVIDENCE PROFILE:
- Victim testimony: Clear, detailed, consistent
- Witness 1 (P.S. - cook): Corroborates victim's distress, confirms assault
- Witness 2 (A.P. - friend of owner): Contradictory account (tried to minimize)
- Misdemeanor verdict: Confirms some charges (Article 10 violation)
- Police involvement: Yes - victim called police, incident documented
- Medical assessment: Not conducted for injury documentation

LEGAL DISTINCTION MADE BY COURT:
Mobing (Article 8a labor law) ≠ Single assault (Article 10 public order) ≠ Criminal harassment
- Misdemeanor: Addresses single public order violation
- Labor law violation: Requires repeated conduct intentionally causing harm/fear
- Criminal harassment: Would require criminal intent + repeated pattern + damage to workplace

CONTRADICTIONS IN TESTIMONY:
- Witness A.P. claimed no drink-pouring observed
- Witness A.P. claimed no physical contact
- Both victim and cook (P.S.) specifically saw drink-pouring incident
- Court found A.P.'s testimony "calculated to ease perpetrator's position"
- Court accepted victim and cook's testimony as credible

COURT COST ASSIGNMENT:
- Victim ordered to pay perpetrator's defense costs: €175.50
- Perpetrator entitled to costs despite acquittal in criminal case
- Victim's request for her advocate costs: DENIED
- Reason: Article 231 ZKP places such costs on represented party (victim)

VICTIM CIVIL REMEDY:
- Victim referred to civil court proceedings (Article 239 ZKP)
- Potential damages claim: Against perpetrator/restaurant owner

CONCLUDING NOTES:
- Case demonstrates distinction between misdemeanor, labor law violations, and criminal harassment
- Single incident, though severe, insufficient for criminal Article 224 conviction
- Pattern requirement is key distinguisher for criminal harassment
- Victim still protected under labor law despite criminal acquittal
- Perpetrator remains subject to labor law discipline even if criminally acquitted

CASE 3: K 292/2014 - BIJELO POLJE
================================================================================
Court: Osnovni Sud u Bijelom Polju
Case Number: K 292/2014
Date of Verdict: 18. Septembar 2015
Judge: Rahmo Banda
Trial Type: ACQUITTAL CASE (Repeated proceeding after appeal)

PERPETRATOR DATA:
- Name: M.B.
- JMBG: [REDACTED]
- Position: Cashier/Treasurer (blagajnik) at Social Services
- Organization: JU D.S. "B.P." (Public Social Services - Bijelo Polje)
- Employment Duration: 15.12.2010 - 01.03.2013 (approximately 2 years)
- Age/Background: Unemployed at time of trial (case involved employment termination)
- Education: Secondary school (SSS)
- Marital Status: Single, never married
- Prior Record: No prior convictions (ne osuđivan)
- Financial Status: Poor (loš imovni status)

VICTIM DATA:
- Name: JU D.S. "B.P." (Public Social Services Institution)
- Victim Type: Organization (institutional victim)
- Representatives: M.H. (institutional representative in court)

INCIDENT CATEGORY:
- Type: Financial irregularities / Embezzlement
- Timeframe: 15.12.2010 - 01.03.2013 (27+ months)
- Nature: Alleged misappropriation of deposits

FINANCIAL ALLEGATIONS:
- Alleged amount embezzled: €8,108.43
- Source: Deposits from service users (clients) held in trust
- Nature of deposits: Personal funds held in custody by social services

PERPETRATOR EMPLOYMENT ROLE:
Job Title: Treasurer/Cashier (blagajnik)
Responsibilities Include:
- Receive cash from service users as deposits
- Keep custody of deposits in safe
- Issue receipts for deposits
- Manage service user accounts
- Process disbursements to users
- Maintain accounting records (if required)

EMPLOYMENT CONDITIONS:
- Type: Fixed-term contracts (repeatedly renewed)
- Employment terms: Stated as "na odredjeno vrijeme" (temporary)
- Job security: Month-to-month renewal basis
- Termination: Ceased 01.03.2013 after investigation

WORK INTERRUPTIONS:
- Illness/Leave Period 1: 16.08.2012 - 02.11.2012 (sick leave)
- Illness/Leave Period 2: 15.11.2012 - 31.01.2013 (sick leave)
- Final period: 11.02.2013 - 01.03.2013 (absent from work)
- Medical status: Had ongoing health issues (on medical leave 6-7 months total)

ALLEGED THEFT MECHANISM:
According to prosecution/expert witness:
1. Perpetrator received deposits from service users
2. Perpetrator issued receipts (supposedly in triplicate)
3. Some deposits allegedly not recorded in accounting system
4. Alleged shortage: €8,108.43 (or €7,308.43 per initial findings)
5. Perpetrator allegedly appropriated these unrecorded funds

PROCEDURAL CONTEXT:
- Initial conviction: K.br.235/2013 dated 03.07.2014
- Initial sentence: 4 months jail
- Appeal by defendant: GRANTED
- Higher court decision: Ukidno rješenje Višeg suda 14.11.2014 - Trial annulled, returned for retrial
- Retrial proceeding: K 292/2014 (current case)
- Retrial date: 18.09.2015

DEFENDANT'S DEFENSE EXPLANATION:
M.B. provided detailed account of work procedures:

Deposit handling procedures per his account:
- No formal written procedure existed for deposit handling
- Procedures conveyed only orally by director and accounting head
- No pravilnik (official regulation) existed for deposit management
- Deposits held in safe received from Center for Social Work (adjacent agency)
- Initial funds came from adjacent social services center at case start
- Perpetrator received safe with single key (other keys missing)
- Later obtained additional key from organization

Initial cash management:
- Told initially to refuse cash deposits (no proper procedures)
- Asked legal adviser for guidance
- Legal adviser contacted director V.D. M.S.
- Decision made to transfer initial deposits to Center for Social Work
- Cash remained at center, later transferred back through multiple individuals

Subsequent deposits:
- Received deposits from various sources:
  * Service users directly
  * Social workers from adjacent service
  * Accounting/administrative staff
  * Director B.K.
- Issued receipts in three copies (one for user, one for cashier, one for social services)
- Performed quarterly/annual inventory checks by commission
- Commission never found shortages in prior years (until final inspection)

Money deposited from Center:
- Perpetrator later withdrew funds from Center for Social Work
- Distributed to service users
- "Razdužio u blagajnu" (recorded/cleared in accounts)

Absence during critical periods:
- When absent, colleague V. J. (female) substituted
- She handled deposits directly to bank during his absence
- She submitted banking receipts
- System maintained parallel to formal accounting

EXPERT WITNESS ASSESSMENT:
Expert: R.V. (ekonomske struke - economic/financial forensics)

Expert findings included:

Initial Report (08.07.2013):
- Reviewed deposit documentation
- Compared receipts to actual cash found (€8,282.35)
- Calculated shortfall: €7,308.43
- Noted institutional failure:
  * No formal procedures for deposits
  * No accounting records kept
  * No annual inventory/verification
  * No designated person responsible for deposits
  * Multiple employees handled deposits (M.B., J. V., P.M., Director B.K.)
  * Incomplete documentation

Supplementary Report (25.06.2014):
- Additional documentation reviewed
- Updated shortage estimate: €1,200.00 (additional amount found)
- Revised total shortage: €8,508.43
- Could not identify individual service users affected by shortage

Additional clarifications (Main trial 18.09.2015):
- Expert acknowledged computational error in original report
- Corrected figures: €8,108.43 (original report had €551.95 vs €151.95 error)
- Expert limitations:
  * Could not identify which service users were short
  * Could not determine what specific fraudulent transactions occurred
  * Could not authenticate signatures on receipts
  * Relied on assumption that all signed receipts represented actual payments
- Documentation challenges:
  * Original receipts not provided (only photocopies)
  * Documentation incomplete and disorganized
  * No continuous accounting records
  * Institutional policies lax/absent

SYSTEMIC PROBLEMS IDENTIFIED:
1. Organizational: No formal deposit procedures
2. Accounting: No ledger/journal system for deposits
3. Verification: No annual inventory checks until crisis
4. Personnel: Multiple people with deposit access
5. Documentation: Incomplete and disorganized
6. Supervision: Minimal oversight during employment

EVIDENCE INTEGRITY ISSUES:
- Original receipts: Not all available for expert review
- Photocopies: Provided instead of originals
- Signatures: Expert could not verify authenticity
- Cash location: Mix of institutional safe and personal locations
- Keys: Multiple keys in question/missing
- Handwriting: Not analyzed for consistency

THIRD WITNESS COMPLICATIONS:
Staff member P.M. (Social Services staff):
- Testified as service user (client of institution)
- Also testified re: cash handling
- Claimed staff informed him he owed 4 months rent (2011)
- Amount: €1,200 (€300/month rate)
- Resolution: P.M. paid €1,200 to clear debt
- His later testimony: "I don't know who made error or where problem is"
- Key point: Confusion about who owed what, when payments made

OTHER RELEVANT TESTIMONY:
- Director K.B. testified that:
  * Relationships became problematic
  * Accounting head and cashier had poor relations
  * Accounting head threatened to transfer M.B. to porter position
  * M.B. frequently absent on sick leave
  * Accounting head (O.M.) managed parallel accounting system
  * Commission conducted annual inventory (tri-party commission per statute)
  * Commission found no shortages in prior years per director

- Bookkeeper J. V. testified:
  * When substituting for M.B., she directly handled deposits
  * Deposited directly to bank on some occasions
  * Two cashier systems appeared to exist (one run by M.B., one by accounting head)
  * No formal decision to have dual cashier system
  * Conflicting procedures existed

DEFENSE ARGUMENTS ON RETRIAL:
Branilac (Defense counsel) K.D.:
1. Objected to prosecution's modification of charges on retrial
   - Claimed modification added elements not in original charge
   - Violated procedural protections ("in dubio pro reo")
   - Prosecution had NOT appealed original acquittal reasoning

2. Original technical issues:
   - Alleged propusti (defects/errors) in original proceeding
   - 12 specific procedural violations cited
   - None remedied in retrial

3. Insufficient evidence:
   - No proof that M.B. specifically embezzled
   - Systemic problems suggest institutional culpability
   - Multiple people with access
   - No clear proof M.B. personally misappropriated funds

4. Alternative explanation:
   - Accounting errors due to poor procedures
   - Some users overpaid, some underpaid
   - Institutional accounting failure ≠ criminal embezzlement

PROSECUTION ARGUMENTS:
Zamjenik ODT (Deputy Prosecutor):
- Maintained original charge with "precision"
- Argued facts fully proven by documentation
- Requested conviction and sentencing per law

COURT'S REASONING FOR ACQUITTAL:
The court found:

1. Burden of proof issue:
   - Principle "in dubio pro reo" applies (when in doubt, benefit defendant)
   - Allegations must be absolutely proven
   - Circumstantial evidence insufficient for conviction

2. Evidentiary problems:
   - Cannot identify specific victims
   - Cannot establish specific fraudulent transactions
   - Cannot prove M.B. personally took funds
   - Multiple people had deposit access
   - Documentation incomplete and contradictory

3. Institutional culpability:
   - Organization failed to establish proper procedures
   - Organization failed to maintain accounting records
   - Organization failed to conduct regular audits
   - Organization allowed dual/parallel accounting systems
   - Accounting head (O.M.) involved in cash handling despite M.B. role

4. Reasonable doubt standard:
   - Expert could only establish general shortage
   - Cannot trace shortage to specific individual actions
   - Cannot prove M.B.'s guilt beyond reasonable doubt
   - Cannot exclude accounting errors or other explanations

5. Procedural context:
   - First trial conviction was problematic
   - Higher court found sufficient issues to retry
   - Current evidence does not overcome reasonable doubt

VERDICT OUTCOME:
- Finding: NOT GUILTY / ACQUITTED
- Basis: Article 373 st.1 tač.2 ZKP (insufficient evidence)
- Reason: "Nije dokazano da je optuženi izvršio krivično djelo" (Not proven defendant committed crime)
- Court costs: Charged to government budget
- Victim (institution): Referred to civil proceedings for recovery

CIVIL REMEDY AVAILABLE:
- Victim institution can pursue civil action
- Can attempt recovery through civil courts
- Would have different evidentiary standard ("preponderance of evidence" vs criminal "beyond reasonable doubt")

KEY LEGAL LESSONS FROM CASE:
1. Criminal conviction requires higher standard than civil liability
2. Systemic organizational failures can cloud individual culpability
3. Embezzlement requires clear proof of specific acts by specific person
4. Incomplete documentation undermines criminal case
5. Multiple access points to funds create reasonable doubt
6. "In dubio pro reo" principle protects defendant when evidence is ambiguous
7. On retrial, prosecution cannot add elements not appealed initially

DEFENDANT'S POST-VERDICT STATUS:
- At time of trial: Unemployed
- Ongoing litigation: Rehabilitation proceeding pending (K.v.br.49/25)
- Health status: Required medical leave during employment, ongoing treatment needs
- Employment prospects: Limited after investigation

INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE:
The institution eventually:
- Hired replacement cashier G.Č. (01.03.2013)
- G.Č. was previously porter (supports director's testimony that M.B. was threatened with demotion)
- Attempted to clarify procedures post-incident

INCIDENT SEQUENCE IN OFFICE:
1. L. Đ. enters, asks director why son denied permanent employment
2. Director responds: "No legal conditions exist; 17 other professors also on fixed-term"
3. K. Z. interjects: Praises director for "feeding my family for 17 years, keeping me employed"

5. Verbal conflict escalates between L. Đ. and director

CRIMINAL CHARGE:
Article 168 st. 1 KZ CG: "Ugrožavanje sigurnosti" (Endangering security)
- Standard definition: Threatening with attack on life/body, causing fear/insecurity
- Requires: Threats causing victim to feel fear and personal endangerment
- Requires: Conduct that objectively appears to threaten attack on life/body

COURT'S LEGAL ANALYSIS:
Key findings:

1. Inconsistent victim testimony:
   - K.Z. very clear on his account
   - But account differs from all other witnesses on critical points
   - Specific threat words NOT corroborated by anyone present
   - Physical contact allegations NOT corroborated by direct witnesses

2. Witness credibility assessment:
   - Director Lj. R.: Under stress, health issues, poor memory
   - D.P.: Partial corroboration (movement toward K.Z.) but no words
   - Š.S.: Contradicts victim on key points
     * Felt no life threat ("Nobody was threatened")
     * Would have remembered specific threats (but didn't)
     * Situation not serious (old man, not dangerous)
     * Hand-to-belt motion appeared uncontrolled/nervous, not threatening
   - L.O. (wife): Contradicts victim version comprehensively
   - L. Đ.: Maintains total denial

3. Temporal issues:
   - Office was small (3x4 meters)
   - If threats made, should have been heard by all
   - Multiple witnesses, only K.Z. recalls specific words
   - Other witnesses focused on broader conflict, not specific threats

4. Firearm relevance:
   - Police found gun at residence LATER
   - Finding proves possession, not use/display on date
   - K.Z. may have learned of gun from police, then incorporated into account
   - Court skeptical that threat included gun references

5. Alternative interpretations:
   - Movement toward K.Z.: Could be directed elsewhere (toward door, toward director)
   - Hand to belt: Could be automatic gesture, not weapon-seeking
   - Emotional tone: L. Đ. genuinely distressed about son's employment situation
   - Š.S. assessment: Stressed reaction rather than threatening conduct

6. Application of Article 168:
Court required analysis of whether Article 168 elements met:
   A. Threat component: Words threatening attack?
      - VICTIM'S VERSION: Yes ("I will judge you", "I will burn you")
      - WITNESS VERSION: No specific threats recalled
      - COURT FINDING: Unclear/unproven
   
   B. Objective threatening conduct: Gestures, positioning suggesting attack?
      - VICTIM'S VERSION: Hand grab + reaching for weapon
      - WITNESS VERSION: Movement occurred, but appeared uncontrolled/nervous
      - COURT FINDING: Movement occurred, but insufficient to establish "threatening"
   
   C. Victim psychological impact: Fear and insecurity caused?
      - VICTIM'S VERSION: Yes, reported calling police, felt threatened
      - WITNESSES: No indication they felt threatened
      - COURT FINDING: Victim subjective fear, but not objectively justified

COURT'S CRITICAL OBSERVATION:
"Sud je odustao od sveho" - Multiple witnesses present
- If such serious threats made with gun references, multiple people should have clearly recalled
- Fact that only victim recalls specific threatening words raises doubt
- Fact that Š.S. (closest witness) says he would have remembered threats if made
- Fact that no witness corroborated specific words suggests:
  * Words not actually said in form alleged, OR
  * Victim misremembered/misinterpreted words, OR
  * Victim fabricated

ACQUITTAL REASONING:
The court found reasonable doubt on whether:
1. Specific threatening words were actually said
2. Physical threats sufficient to constitute Article 168 offense
3. Victim's fear was reasonable reaction to perpetrator's conduct
4. Conduct crossed threshold from heated argument to criminal threatening

Court applied "in dubio pro reo" (when in doubt, benefit defendant)
- Allegations must be clearly proven beyond reasonable doubt
- Inconsistency between victim account and other witnesses creates doubt
- Isolated incident during emotional/stressful confrontation
- No pattern of threatening conduct documented

VERDICT OUTCOME:
- Finding: NOT GUILTY / ACQUITTED
- Basis: Article 373 tač. 2 ZKP (insufficient evidence)
- Reason: Facts/charges in indictment not proven beyond reasonable doubt
- Court costs: Stated as "nije bilo" (there were no/minimal costs)
- Victim reparation: Referred to civil court proceedings
- No criminal consequences
- Criminal record: None

KEY LEGAL LESSONS FROM CASE:
1. Multiple witness accounts matter more than single alleged victim
2. Inconsistencies in victim testimony create reasonable doubt
3. Stress/emotions during argument not same as criminal threatening
4. Threat must be specific, clear, and corroborated
5. Firearm possession ≠ threatening use of firearm
6. Court skeptical of victim account completely contradicted by others
7. Isolated incident during heated employment dispute insufficient
8. "In dubio pro reo" protects even unpopular defendants

ACQUITTAL SIGNIFICANCE:
- While L. Đ. may have been rude, course, or emotionally reactive
- His conduct did not cross legal threshold into Article 168 criminal threat
- Jury/judgment rule: When evidence unclear, defendant benefits
- Victim had other remedies (civil suit) but not proven for criminal conviction
